Field notes: where all this started

FRP stands for Fiberglass Reinforced Plastic. What launched modern composites in the first place was phenolic resin. Fiberglass boatbuilding itself began after WWII, when the US Navy contracted a 28-foot personnel craft built from it, chasing reduced maintenance versus wood — and it proliferated around 1960 as lower hull costs and near-zero maintenance drove a boom in ownership.

Early FRP builders had no structural calculator to lean on. They used the empirical method — build it, test if it survives — which is exactly why so many early fiberglass boats are over-built: without a calculated design basis, builders defaulted to conservative (excess) material. The driving factor behind FRP's development in the first place was the ability to produce complex hull configurations and the advantages of a seamless, moldable hull versus planked wood.

Three advantages composites hold over traditional materials: higher specific strength (strength per pound), the ability to be formed into complex shapes (design flexibility), and greater durability.
