Recap you already own

Secondary bonding is the whole game in repair

Module 3's one-way bond street applies directly to repair work — run the interactive version there if you want it again. The short version: epoxy has the best secondary-bonding characteristics of the three resin families, and bonds well to nearly everything, including cured polyester or vinylester. Polyester and vinylester, on the other hand, bond poorly to fully-cured epoxy without heavy prep. That's why epoxy is the default resin for repair and secondary bonding.

And the green window matters just as much in repair as it did in the shop: while a laminate is still curing ("green"), a fresh layer can chemically bond to it. Once it's fully cured, a new layer only gets a weaker secondary/mechanical bond — which means the surface needs sanding and a solvent wipe before you laminate over it. Every repair you do is bonding to fully-cured, pre-existing laminate, so this prep step is never optional.

Recap from Module 11: if a laminate has been affected by drainage or moisture, the move is the same every time: inspect the extent of the damage, remove any trapped moisture, then repair the affected area. You'll see that exact sequence again in the blister-repair panel below.

The geometry of a strong patch

12:1 — and never smaller than 6 inches

Repairing a single-skin hole means tapering ("scarfing") back the surrounding laminate so new plies overlap old ones gradually instead of butting into a hard step. Slide the laminate thickness and watch the taper.

The two numbers to know cold: a single-skin hole repair uses a 12:1 scarf ratio — that's both the typical and the minimum. And no matter how small the damage, the finished scarf diameter shouldn't be less than about 6 inches. On thin laminate the 12:1 math alone can call for less than that — the 6-inch floor overrides it.
Formica as a splash mold: when repairing a hole straight through a single skin, Formica is commonly used as a splash mold — a smooth, non-stick backing surface you can rebuild the outer contour and gelcoat surface against from behind the hole.
